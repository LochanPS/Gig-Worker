PROJECT: GIGBRIDGE — AUTONOMOUS CROSS-BORDER FREELANCER PAYMENT GATEWAY

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. ONE-LINE SUMMARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
GigBridge is an autonomous, AI-driven cross-border payment gateway that lets a
company anywhere in the world pay a freelancer anywhere else in the world
directly — with automated identity verification, jurisdiction-aware regulatory
compliance, real-time currency conversion, and blockchain-based settlement —
in minutes instead of 3-5 business days, and at a fraction of the 8-10% fees
charged by traditional intermediaries like PayPal or Western Union.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. PROBLEM STATEMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 The situation
Freelancers across the globe take on tasks from companies in other countries.
The company-freelancer relationship already exists; we are NOT building a
marketplace to connect them. The friction begins at the moment of payment.

2.2 The pain
When a German company pays an Indian freelancer:
  a) The payment passes through payment agencies, correspondent banks, and
     currency-exchange middlemen.
  b) These intermediaries take 8-10% of the payment in fees — devastating for
     small gig payments where the absolute amounts are already low.
  c) Verification, compliance clearance, and settlement take 3-5 business days.
  d) Exchange rates fluctuate daily; the freelancer bears rate risk and often
     receives less than expected.
  e) Both sides must navigate two regulatory regimes (e.g. RBI/FEMA in India,
     BaFin/ECB/GDPR in the EU, FinCEN in the US) with no automation.

2.3 The consequence
Freelancers lose a large slice of small payments, wait days for money they have
already earned, and lose motivation to take international gig work. Companies
(especially startups relying entirely on freelancers) face unpredictable costs,
delays, and compliance uncertainty.

2.4 The problem in one sentence
How can an autonomous system facilitate compliant, low-cost, near-instant,
direct cross-border payments between companies and freelancers while
dynamically handling exchange rates and regulations of both countries?

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. WHO WE ARE SOLVING FOR (USERS AND PERSONAS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1 Persona A — The Freelancer ("Priya", Bengaluru, India)
  - Takes small-to-medium gigs ($50 - $5,000) from foreign companies.
  - Pain: loses 8-10% to intermediaries, waits 3-5 days, re-does KYC for
    every platform, gets poor FX rates.
  - Wants: full payment, fast, in INR, in her bank account, with a clean
    record for her own tax filing (FIRC-equivalent proof of foreign income).

3.2 Persona B — The Company ("Novatek GmbH", Berlin, Germany)
  - A startup with no full-time staff, running entirely on global freelancers.
  - Pain: every payout is a manual banking chore; compliance is a black box;
    fees are unpredictable; finance has no dashboard of freelancer payouts.
  - Wants: one-click payout after approving work, guaranteed compliance in
    both jurisdictions, batch payouts, invoices/receipts auto-generated.

3.3 Persona C — The Platform Operator (us — GigBridge admin/ops)
  - Runs the gateway as a business.
  - Needs: monitoring of all transactions, compliance-flag review queue,
    liquidity/treasury view, fraud alerts, revenue dashboard.

3.4 Why they would pay for this
  - Freelancer: keeps ~7-9% more of every payment and gets it 100x faster.
  - Company: predictable flat low fee, automated compliance paperwork,
    one dashboard for all global payouts.
  - Operator: earns a thin transparent fee (0.5-1% or flat fee) at volume —
    honest economics vs. the hidden FX-spread model of incumbents.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. THE SOLUTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.1 Concept
An "Autonomous Cross-Border Financial Agent" that sits between payer and payee
and does — automatically, in seconds — everything the 3-5 day intermediary
chain does manually:

  Company initiates payment
    -> Agent verifies both parties (KYC for freelancer, KYB for company,
       verified once and reused via decentralized identity credentials)
    -> Agent runs a jurisdiction-aware compliance rule engine on BOTH the
       sender's and receiver's country regulations (limits, purpose codes,
       sanctions screening, tax notes)
    -> Agent locks a real-time FX rate with a validity window
    -> Funds move through a blockchain settlement layer (stablecoin rail,
       e.g. EUR -> USDC -> INR) via an escrow smart contract
    -> Payout lands with the freelancer (wallet in demo; bank off-ramp
       partner in production), with a full audit trail and auto-generated
       compliance documents for both sides.

4.2 What makes it different from PayPal / Western Union / Wise
  - Autonomy: an AI agent makes compliance determinations and orchestrates the
    pipeline end-to-end; humans only review flagged edge cases.
  - Verify-once identity: reusable verifiable credentials mean the 3-5 day
    "clarification" delay happens once per user, not once per payment.
  - Transparent pricing: flat low fee + mid-market FX rate shown up front;
    no hidden spread.
  - Blockchain settlement: minutes, not days; immutable audit trail satisfies
    both regulators and auditors.
  - Two-way by design: EU->India, India->EU, US->India, India->US, etc.

4.3 Explicit non-goals (hackathon scope)
  - Not a freelancer marketplace (no job discovery/matching).
  - Not a real money-transmitter license holder (production requires licensed
    partners; demo simulates the fiat edges).
  - Not global coverage on day one: demo corridors are EUR<->INR and USD<->INR.

5. FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FR-1  Onboarding & Identity
  FR-1.1  Freelancer sign-up with KYC flow (document upload simulated;
          verification status machine: pending -> verified -> rejected).
  FR-1.2  Company sign-up with KYB flow (registration number, country,
          authorized signatory).
  FR-1.3  On successful verification, issue a Verifiable Credential (VC)
          bound to a DID; store hash on-chain, PII off-chain (GDPR).
  FR-1.4  Credential reuse: subsequent payments skip re-verification;
          credentials carry expiry and revocation status.

FR-2  Payment Lifecycle
  FR-2.1  Company creates a payout: payee, amount, source currency, target
          currency, purpose (e.g. "software services"), optional invoice ref.
  FR-2.2  Escrow option: company can fund escrow at gig start; release on
          work approval.
  FR-2.3  Payment states: DRAFT -> COMPLIANCE_CHECK -> RATE_LOCKED ->
          FUNDED -> SETTLING -> COMPLETED (or FLAGGED / REJECTED / REFUNDED).
  FR-2.4  Freelancer can also raise an invoice/payment request that the
          company approves with one click.
  FR-2.5  Batch payouts (company pays N freelancers in one action).

FR-3  Autonomous Compliance Agent
  FR-3.1  Rule engine evaluates every payment against BOTH jurisdictions:
          - India: RBI/FEMA purpose codes, LRS-style limits, PAN presence,
            export-of-services classification, TDS note.
          - EU: AMLD thresholds, GDPR data handling, sanctions lists.
          - US: OFAC screening, FinCEN thresholds.
  FR-3.2  Sanctions/watchlist screening of both parties (mock list in demo).
  FR-3.3  Decision output: APPROVE / FLAG_FOR_REVIEW / REJECT, with a
          human-readable reasoning trace (the agent explains itself).
  FR-3.4  Flagged payments enter the admin review queue; admin can approve
          with justification or reject; everything is logged.
  FR-3.5  LLM-powered "compliance explainer": for any payment, the agent
          produces a plain-English summary of which rules applied and why
          the decision was made (demo wow-factor + genuine utility).
  FR-3.6  Anomaly detection: velocity checks (too many payments/day),
          amount outliers vs. payee history, structuring detection
          (splitting to dodge thresholds).

FR-4  FX Engine
  FR-4.1  Pull real-time mid-market rates (live API with cached fallback).
  FR-4.2  Rate lock at initiation with a 10-minute validity window; if
          settlement exceeds window, re-quote and require re-confirm.
  FR-4.3  Show full fee breakdown before confirmation: mid-market rate,
          platform fee, network/gas estimate, exact amount payee receives.
  FR-4.4  Historic rate chart per corridor on the dashboards.

FR-5  Settlement Layer
  FR-5.1  Escrow smart contract: fund, release-on-approval, refund,
          dispute-freeze. Deployed on an EVM testnet (local Anvil chain for
          the live demo; Polygon Amoy/Sepolia as public proof).
  FR-5.2  Stablecoin leg: mock USDC (own ERC-20 for demo) as the transfer
          medium; fiat edges simulated by on-ramp/off-ramp mock services.
  FR-5.3  Every settlement emits events; the UI shows a live on-chain
          transaction timeline with tx hashes and block confirmations.
  FR-5.4  Immutable audit log: compliance decision hash + payment hash
          anchored on-chain.

FR-6  Dashboards (three, matching the three personas)
  FR-6.1  COMPANY DASHBOARD
          - Overview: total paid, pending, in-escrow, fees saved vs. PayPal.
          - "New Payout" wizard (payee -> amount -> live quote -> compliance
            pre-check -> confirm) — the demo centerpiece.
          - Payout table with state chips and drill-down timeline.
          - Freelancer roster with verification badges.
          - Escrow management, batch payout, invoices inbox.
  FR-6.2  FREELANCER DASHBOARD
          - Balance and incoming payments with live status timeline
            (each stage ticks off in real time during the demo).
          - Earnings history, FX rate at settlement, fee breakdown,
            "you kept 99.X%" banner.
          - Raise invoice, manage payout preferences (hold USD vs.
            auto-convert INR), verification/credential status card.
  FR-6.3  ADMIN / OPERATOR DASHBOARD
          - Live transaction monitor across the whole platform.
          - Compliance review queue with the agent's reasoning per case.
          - Fraud/anomaly alerts, sanctions-hit review.
          - Corridor liquidity + treasury view, revenue dashboard.
          - Rule-engine editor view (list of active rules per country).

FR-7  Notifications & Documents
  FR-7.1  In-app + email-style notifications on every state change.
  FR-7.2  Auto-generated documents: payment receipt, fee statement,
          foreign-inward-remittance certificate equivalent (demo PDF),
          compliance report per transaction.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. NON-FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
NFR-1  Demo latency: full payment lifecycle visibly completes in < 60 seconds.
NFR-2  Compliance decisions deterministic + reproducible (rules are code;
       LLM only explains, never decides alone).
NFR-3  PII never on-chain; only hashes/attestations (GDPR by design).
NFR-4  All services runnable locally with one command (docker compose) —
       the demo must not die if the venue Wi-Fi does.
NFR-5  Auditability: every state transition logged with actor + timestamp.
NFR-6  Security basics: JWT auth, role-based access (company/freelancer/
       admin), input validation, no secrets in repo.

7. SUCCESS METRICS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Hackathon:
  - End-to-end live demo: create payout -> compliance -> rate lock -> on-chain
    escrow -> release -> freelancer sees money, in under a minute.
  - Judges can see the agent's reasoning trace and the on-chain tx hash.
  - Fee comparison banner: "PayPal: $48.20 on $500. GigBridge: $3.75."

  - Settlement time minutes vs. 3-5 days.
  - Verify-once: repeat payment requires zero re-verification.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. BUSINESS MODEL (POST-HACKATHON PATH)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8.1 Revenue
  - Flat 0.5-1% transaction fee (capped), transparently shown.
  - Premium company plan: batch payouts, API access, accounting integrations.
  - Float/treasury yield on escrow balances (where legally permitted).
  - Compliance-report generation as a paid add-on for accountants.
8.2 Go-to-market
  - Start with the India<->EU and India<->US corridors (largest freelancer
    flows), target startup companies paying 5-50 freelancers/month.
8.3 Regulatory path to production
  - Partner with licensed entities instead of becoming one first:
    - India off-ramp: AD-1 bank / licensed PA-CB (Payment Aggregator
      Cross-Border) partner.
    - EU on-ramp: EMI-licensed partner (or agent-of model).
    - Stablecoin leg via a regulated custodian (e.g. licensed VASP).
  - Long term: obtain own PA-CB license in India, EMI in EU.
8.4 Moat
  - The compliance rule engine + agent reasoning corpus per corridor is the
    accumulating asset; each new corridor added compounds coverage that
    incumbents price at 8-10% and we price at <1%.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. DEMO CORRIDORS AND SCENARIO (WHAT THE JUDGES SEE)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Scenario 1 (happy path): Novatek GmbH (Berlin) pays Priya (Bengaluru) EUR 500
for a completed gig. Wizard shows live EUR/INR quote and fee breakdown;
compliance agent approves with visible reasoning; escrow funds on-chain;
release; Priya's dashboard timeline completes; receipt PDF generated.
Scenario 2 (compliance catch): A payment to a sanctioned mock entity gets
REJECTED; a payment above threshold without purpose code gets FLAGGED and
appears in the admin queue with the agent's explanation; admin resolves it.
Scenario 3 (reverse direction): Indian company pays a US freelancer USD 1,200 —
proving two-way, multi-jurisdiction capability.
Scenario 4 (fraud): rapid-fire split payments trigger the structuring alarm
on the admin dashboard.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10. OPEN QUESTIONS / ASSUMPTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A-1  Fiat edges (bank in/out) are simulated in the demo; stated honestly as
     "licensed partner integration" in the pitch.
A-2  KYC document verification is mocked (instant verify) but the state
     machine and credential issuance are real.
A-3  Regulatory rules implemented are a representative, defensible subset of
     RBI/FEMA, EU AMLD/GDPR, US OFAC — demonstrating the architecture, not
     claiming legal completeness.
A-4  Blockchain runs on a local/test network; zero real-money risk.
